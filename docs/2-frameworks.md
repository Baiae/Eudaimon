# 2. Frameworks in the loop

The AGI+W blueprint uses six conceptual frameworks. They are expressed here as
interfaces and review questions, not as implemented cognitive faculties.

## 1. General intelligence

The campaign contract permits different proposal generators and evaluators.
The engine knows nothing about a model architecture, prompting style, or
domain-specific harness.

## 2. Wisdom

Wisdom is treated as a constrained objective: useful improvement must remain
inside explicit protective and human-review boundaries. It is not inferred from
the score and is not represented by a consciousness claim.

## 3. Reflection

The ledger and held-out split provide material for after-action inspection.
They do not make the system self-aware; they make decisions reconstructable.

## 4. Deliberation

The evaluator may expose multiple component scores, but the engine consumes a
single scalar `S` after the Protect Gate. This keeps the acceptance rule
deterministic and makes trade-offs visible in the evaluator output.

## 5. Social and human alignment

The blueprint reserves human sign-off for changes to Protect or halt
conditions, and for other high-consequence calibration changes. The reference
engine does not silently grant that authority to a proposal or model.

## 6. Self-improvement

AutoResearch-W improves a campaign target through externally supplied
proposals. The engine does not invent a proposal, rewrite its own evaluator,
or modify its own halt rules. Those boundaries are intentional until a human
review process is specified.

## Review boundary

These framework mappings are functional analogues. They are useful for
designing tests and interfaces, but they are not evidence that the resulting
software has general intelligence, wisdom, agency, or subjective experience.
